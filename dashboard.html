Mortgage Rates & the Macro Backdrop

Mortgage rates roughly track the 10-year Treasury + inflation + Fed tone, with a lag. Watching the 10-yr and CPI tells you where the 30-yr fixed is likely headed.

Affordability iAffordability index. The monthly principal & interest payment on the area's median home (20% down, current 30-yr rate) as a share of the area's median household income. Above ~30% is historically "stretched." When payments are stretched, prices have less room to keep rising and more room to fall; when affordability is normal, prices are nearer a floor.

Seasonality & Timing Windows

Two calendar patterns to exploit: builder quarter-ends (Mar / Jun / Sep / Dec) are when production builders discount hardest to hit sales targets, and spring brings the most inventory but also the most competing buyers. Late fall / winter has thinner inventory but far less competition and more motivated sellers.

How to read this dashboard

Leading vs. lagging. Inventory, days-on-market, price cuts and sale-to-list are leading — they shift first. Median price and YoY are lagging — they confirm a move that already happened. Watch the leading set to see a turn coming; use price to confirm it.

Why inventory moves before price. Sellers cut price only after their home sits. So supply rising and DOM lengthening show up weeks-to-months before the median sale price bends. By the time YoY price is clearly negative, the leverage shift is already well underway.

Why nobody times the exact bottom. You only know the bottom in the rear-view mirror, and a ~9% round-trip in transaction costs (agent commissions, closing, moving, seller costs when you eventually sell) dwarfs the money you'd save shaving a small amount off the price or rate. A rate you dislike can be refinanced later; the price you pay is locked in. Aim for a good window with real leverage, not a perfect one.
